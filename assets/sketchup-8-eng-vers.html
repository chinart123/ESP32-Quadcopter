Hardware Platform & Interfaces
  • Central processor: ESP32-S3 Supermini (Dual-Core 240 MHz) — enough headroom for floating-point matrix math.

  • Actuation: 8520 coreless motors, 4 kHz PWM through MOSFET drivers.

Technical Challenges vs. Core Solutions
1
Mechanical vibration noise: vibration from the 8520 motors corrupts the accelerometer, causing angle drift.  →  2-axis Kalman filter
2
Computational bottleneck: running positioning matrices and PID together stalls the MCU.  →  Dual-Core FreeRTOS
3
Ground effect: reflected airflow breaks the linearity of a single-loop PID.  →  Cascade PI-D

These three challenges are the roadmap for the entire theory section that follows.

Dynamics: Attitude–Altitude Coupling

The total thrust vector from the 4 motors is always normal to the body frame. When the drone tilts to translate (Roll $\phi$, Pitch $\theta$), the projection of thrust onto the vertical Z axis drops instantly → altitude loss unless compensated.

$$ u_z(t) = \frac{T_{total}\cdot\cos(\phi)\cdot\cos(\theta)}{m} - g $$

The system needs continuous feedforward compensation against tilt — this is the root of the altitude-hold problem.

Real-Time Multitasking (FreeRTOS)

Solving bottleneck #2: activate both asymmetric cores of the ESP32-S3.

CORE 1 · ATTITUDE LOOP — 250 Hz (4 ms)
  • Read the IMU MPU6050 & run the Kalman filter.
  • Compute the angular-rate PID.
  • Mix signals & output PWM directly to the 4 motors.
CORE 0 · NAV LOOP — 100 Hz (10 ms)
  • Receive RC commands over ELRS (UART).
  • Read ToF & optical flow.
  • Run the Altitude/Position PID, feed set-points to Core 1.

The Big Picture: Sensor Fusion
1
Geometric Projection: the ToF beam reads slanted under tilt → multiply by $\cos(\text{Roll})\cdot\cos(\text{Pitch})$ to recover the true perpendicular height $Z_{true}$.
2
Height Scaling: multiply the optical-flow pixel displacement by the true altitude $Z_{true}$ to obtain velocity in m/s.
3
Gyro Compensation: subtract the virtual pixel motion caused by the drone's own rotation (from the gyroscope) to extract pure translational velocity.

Algorithm Flow: IMU & Kalman Filter

Task: reject mechanical-vibration noise on the accelerometer to extract a clean tilt angle. The 2-axis Kalman filter continuously optimizes the error covariance matrix $P_k$.

Predict

$$ \hat{x}_{k|k-1} = F\hat{x}_{k-1} + Bu_k $$
$$ P_{k|k-1} = FP_{k-1}F^T + Q $$

Update — gain $K_k$

$$ K_k = P_{k|k-1}H^T(HP_{k|k-1}H^T + R)^{-1} $$
$$ \hat{x}_k = \hat{x}_{k|k-1} + K_k(z_k - H\hat{x}_{k|k-1}) $$

Altitude Sensor: ToF (VL53L1X)

Geometric problem: when the body tilts, the ToF laser measures along the hypotenuse → the apparent distance exceeds reality, so the system mistakes it for the drone climbing.

Geometric Projection: use the Kalman-filtered Euler angles to project the diagonal vector back onto the true perpendicular height.

$$ Z_{true} = D_{ToF\_Raw}\cdot\cos(\phi_{Roll})\cdot\cos(\theta_{Pitch}) $$

Optical-Flow Sensor (PMW3901) — XY Positioning

Task: track XY drift for the Position-Hold PID loop. The sensor only counts pixel slip $\Delta px$; the real velocity depends on altitude and must be cleared of body-rotation noise.

$$ V_{x} = \left(\frac{\Delta px_x}{\Delta t}\right)\cdot\frac{Z_{true}}{k_{opt}} - (\omega_{gyro\_y}\cdot Z_{true}) $$

Height Scaling uses $Z_{true}$ (from ToF) to convert pixels → m/s; Gyro Compensation subtracts the virtual velocity from body rotation. The resulting $V_x, V_y$ feed directly into the outer loop.

Cascade Control Architecture (Cascade PI-D)

To fully damp the ground effect, the system uses two nested loops:

OUTER LOOP (PI) — slow / accurate

Drives position $Z, XY$ to the target → outputs a target velocity.

$$ v_{target} = K_{p1}e_{pos} + K_{i1}\!\int e_{pos}\,dt $$
INNER LOOP (PD) — fast / strong

Forces velocity to track $v_{target}$ at 250 Hz, rejecting gusts instantly.

$$ u_{motor} = K_{p2}(v_{target}-v_{real}) - K_{d2}\dot{v}_{real} $$

Limitations & Operating Conditions

The main limitation comes from the low-cost PMW3901 optical-flow sensor — which imposes strict requirements on surface and lighting:

FLOOR SURFACE

Needs a random, chaotic texture. A regular repeating surface (periodic tile patterns, smooth floors) leaves the sensor unable to distinguish motion → position hold is lost. Workaround: print a noisy random pattern on floor paper.

LIGHTING

Avoid overly bright / glary environments. Reflective surfaces (glass) and outdoor sunlight disturb both optical flow and ToF. Room lights must not cast hard shadows — otherwise the drone cannot read the black/white contrast.

Scope: indoor, calm air, controlled lighting & surface · point hover only (no waypoints yet) · ~5.5 min per charge. A deliberate trade-off to keep cost < $50.

Conclusion: Contribution & Future Work

Our contribution: integrating Geometric Projection for ToF + optical-flow fusion (height scaling & gyro compensation) running in real time on a < $50 MCU, with an asymmetric dual-core architecture — reaching ±2 cm altitude hold and ±4 cm position hold without LiDAR or a vision computer.

  • Fully solves the indoor 6-DOF problem in a GPS-denied, low-cost setting.
  • An open-source, stable platform — ready to host a block-programming interface.

Future work: build a Blockly/Scratch API library for K-12 students, turning the drone into a STEM teaching tool.

[Backup] ToF Sensor Exception Handling

Edge case: when the drone slides hard and tilt exceeds $\ge 15^\circ$, the laser reflects off a wall/obstacle instead of the floor → a steep error spike.

// Fail-safe: freeze the reading & lock the integrator if ( |Roll| ≥ 15° OR |Pitch| ≥ 15° ) { Freeze_Filter(Z_true); // freeze the measured value Lock_PID_Integral(); // anti-windup }

Additionally: if tilt exceeds $40^\circ$, the firmware automatically triggers an emergency cut-off to protect the motors.
